What the validator proves:
- every number in a plan traces to a Zetaris metric, to a projection whose arithmetic it recomputes, or to
  a lever the plan declares as a proposed choice;
- salon and online numbers aren't blended without a labeled combined metric.

What it doesn't prove: that an assumption (e.g. "a 20% price rise lifts revenue 40%") is wise. That
judgment stays with the people who approve the plan.
