# Demo video script (rule 6.1: 1 to 4 minutes)

The voice-over of the submitted video, copied from the final pack. 3:50 planned, under the 4:00 limit. Ezra's phone stands
in for every head; keys, tokens, phone numbers and codes are never on screen. All data is synthetic.

| Time | Segment | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:17 | 1. The problem | My family's company is two businesses: my parents' salon in Brooklyn and Lakewood, and my online wig store. Our numbers sit in a dozen systems that never talk. I want one place that tells me what I need to make profitable decisions. |
| 0:17 to 0:34 | 2. The company, run like an enterprise | So I organized it like an enterprise. Four divisions. Cindy manages the Salon for my mother, Esther. George manages Finance for my father, Alan. Georgette manages Online for Emma. And Joey is my CEO assistant. The AI managers read and advise. The people decide. |
| 0:34 to 0:58 | 3. Zetaris: a dozen sources, one governed picture | Zetaris is the only way any assistant reads a number. It federates our separately owned sources where they live, with no copies. Each division gets its own Virtual Data Mart: the salon never sees online numbers. On the first twelve sources, all 145 checks matched an independent computation; on the full build, every division's read matched it too. |
| 0:58 to 1:19 | 4. One real request through Meterless Nightshift | Every model call goes through Meterless Nightshift, our primary AI provider. Nightshift holds the NVIDIA key and calls NVIDIA for us. Here's one real request: Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, our front door, answering through Nightshift. Only the safety screen calls NVIDIA directly, because Nightshift doesn't serve that model yet. |
| 1:19 to 1:47 | 5. The morning: fifteen workers, three managers, one ask across a wall | Every morning fifteen workers each watch one part of the business; thirteen have their data connected so far. A program sets each status from fixed rules. Nemotron 3 Ultra writes the words, and a number that doesn't trace back to Zetaris is refused. Online can't read Amazon stock, so Georgette asks George. Each week George gives Alan a goal and three profit paths, each with its math, a test and a stop-loss. |
| 1:47 to 2:02 | 6. The weekly packet: PDF by email, through Postmark | The packet goes out by email through Postmark. For the demo it comes to my inbox, standing in for Alan's. The PDF has the goal, the paths and a page per worker. The email never carries an approval code. |
| 2:02 to 2:17 | 7. A head approves by one-time code | Alan decides with a one-time code. A plain OK never counts. The code is single-use, tied to him and to this report. The program records his choice. It doesn't move money or change an ad. Agents propose; people decide. |
| 2:17 to 2:32 | 8. The CEO digest from Joey | And I get one short text from Joey. One line per division: running normally, needs a look, or needs a decision. A program writes it, not a model, so it can't add a number of its own. |
| 2:32 to 2:51 | 9. Meterless memory: the World Model and H-MEM | Underneath, two Meterless engines. The World Model records who asked, what was proposed and who decided, so 'why plan 2?' is answered from the record itself. H-MEM is each assistant's memory. An idea stays short-term until a person approves it, so an unapproved idea is never remembered as a decision. |
| 2:51 to 3:11 | 10. The HQ Manager applies a lesson by itself | The HQ Manager reviews everyone's work. With my approval, it applies a lesson by itself and tells me what changed. Every change is a new profile version in Supabase that I can put back. Anything from an outside tool, or a change to what an assistant reads, waits for my code. |
| 3:11 to 3:32 | 11. The Legal Team, reached from Claude Code through a door | Staff use their own AI tools too. Here Claude Code opens the Legal Team through a read-only door. It marks each clause green, yellow or red, says when a real attorney must look, and files a report back. The HQ Manager reads it, but anything from an outside tool waits for my code. |
| 3:32 to 3:50 | 12. Close: impact and what's next | All the data here is synthetic, but the problem is real. One family, two businesses, a dozen systems, and now one short text that tells me where to look. Next, this goes into our operations hub, one division at a time. |
